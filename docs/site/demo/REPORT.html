What marks a fraudulent transaction?

fraud_200k.csv · 200,000 rows
Your question
What are the characteristics of fraudulent transactions?

The answer

In this PaySim sample, fraud shows up only in two transaction types. All 240 fraudulent transactions are TRANSFER or CASH_OUT: 0.7% of transfers and 0.2% of cash-outs, against 0.0% for PAYMENT, CASH_IN and DEBIT.

Written by the AI from the analysis; every number was checked against it.

What we found

Fraud occurs only in transfers and cash-outs; payments, cash-ins and debits carry none.

Clear evidence
Show more

TRANSFER has a fraud rate of 0.7% (95% interval 0.6% to 0.9%, n=16,898) and CASH_OUT 0.2% (0.1% to 0.2%, n=70,084). PAYMENT (n=67,660), CASH_IN (n=43,991) and DEBIT (n=1,367) show 0.0%. Restricting to TRANSFER and CASH_OUT still keeps all 240 fraud cases (86,982 rows), which matches the brief's description of fraudsters depleting accounts through transfers and cash-outs.

Be careful:

  • DEBIT is small, so its interval reaches 0.3%.

All the numbers behind it: F1 in details/1_analysis.pdf

Fraudulent transactions are typically much larger, and the fraud rate climbs steeply with amount.

Clear evidence
Show more

The median fraud amount is 401,192 against 73,996 for legitimate transactions (standardised difference +0.74). The fraud rate is 0.0% below 10,000, 0.1% from 10,000 to 200,000 and 0.2% from 200,000 to 1,000,000. It then jumps to 1.8% for 1,000,000 to below 5,000,000 and 4.7% at 5,000,000 and over (interval 3.0% to 7.4%, n=360). Still, 155 of the 240 fraud cases are below 1,000,000, so most fraud is not in the high bands.

Be careful:

  • Part of this gap reflects type mix: PAYMENT and CASH_IN are mostly small and carry no fraud.

All the numbers behind it: F2 in details/1_analysis.pdf

Large amounts mark fraud among cash-outs, but not among transfers.

Clear evidence
Show more

Within CASH_OUT, fraud amounts have a median of 455,710 against 146,796 (standardised difference +0.76). Cash-outs of 1,000,000 and over are 77.8% fraudulent (interval 65.1% to 86.8%, n=54), against 0.2% or less below that amount. Within TRANSFER, fraudulent amounts are if anything smaller (median 371,771 against 485,695; standardised difference +0.23 on means). The TRANSFER fraud rate is 1.2% below 100,000, 0.4% from 200,000 to 1,000,000 and 1.0% at 1,000,000 and over.

Be careful:

  • The CASH_OUT group at 1,000,000 and over is only 54 rows, so the exact share is imprecise. 9 rows in the larger CASH_OUT bands were too few to show.

All the numbers behind it: F3 in details/1_analysis.pdf

Among transfers and cash-outs, type, timing and amount each stay associated with fraud when the others are held fixed.

Clear evidence
Show more

In a model limited to TRANSFER and CASH_OUT (86,982 rows, 240 fraud), TRANSFER goes with x3.67 the odds of fraud against CASH_OUT (2.82–4.77). Each 100 steps later goes with x1.68 (1.56–1.82), and each extra 1,000,000 with x1.12 (1.08–1.16), all p<0.001. The model clearly beats no model (p<0.001; AUC 0.76 on the same rows). In the full model over all types, the zero-fraud types got x0.00 with intervals reaching infinity.

Be careful:

  • The full model (T8) warned: the model did not settle on stable estimates, often because a category has only one outcome, and effects with very wide intervals are unreliable. Only the restricted model (T11) is used for conclusions.
  • Amount enters as a straight line. This understates the sharp jump among cash-outs of 1,000,000 and over and averages the opposite patterns in the two types.
  • The step effect largely mirrors the late-month volume drop (F4).
  • AUC is measured on the same rows the model was fitted on.

What you could do

  • Treat transfers and cash-outs as the only relevant population for any fraud description or model of this data; the other types carry no fraud here.
  • Describe amount separately for each type. For cash-outs, 1,000,000 and over is a strong marker; for transfers, amount alone tells little.
  • Do not use step as a fraud signal without first adjusting for transaction volume per step, since the rise in rate reflects the simulator's volume pattern.
